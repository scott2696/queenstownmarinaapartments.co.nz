What RTP actually means

RTP is the percentage of all wagered money a pokie is designed to pay back over the long run. A 98% RTP means that across millions of spins, the game returns NZ$98 for every NZ$100 staked — the other NZ$2 is the house edge. It says nothing about your next spin or your session tonight; it's the long-run average that holds across huge volumes of play.

RTP vs volatility — both matter

  • RTP = how much comes back over time. Higher is better for your bankroll.
  • Volatility = how the returns arrive. Low-volatility pokies (Blood Suckers, Starmania) pay small and often; high-volatility pokies pay rarely but big.

For the longest play on a set budget, pair a high RTP with low volatility. For jackpot-chasing, you accept lower RTP and higher volatility.

RTP and the house edge

RTP and house edge are two sides of one coin. If a pokie's RTP is 98%, its house edge is 2% — that 2% is what the casino keeps over the long run. A 94% pokie hands the house three times the edge of a 98% one, which is why chasing high RTP genuinely matters over many sessions even though it can't rescue any single one. Table games make this vivid: single-zero (European) roulette returns about 97.3% versus American roulette's ~94.7%, so the wheel you pick changes your expected loss before you place a chip.

How to check RTP before you play

  1. Open the pokie and tap the info / paytable icon.
  2. Find the "RTP" or "theoretical return" line.
  3. Confirm it matches the headline figure — if it's a couple of points lower, the casino is running a reduced-RTP version; consider switching casinos.

We don't lift RTP numbers off other tables. Each figure here is the studio's published theoretical return, cross-checked against the paytable in a live NZ-facing casino. Where a game ships in more than one configuration — Mega Joker's 99% only applies at max bet, for instance, and several titles have 96% and 94% variants — we note the caveat rather than quoting the best-case number as if it were guaranteed. High RTP is one factor, not the whole picture

A high return keeps your bankroll in play longer, but it doesn't override the rest of a smart approach. Set a budget you're happy to lose and stop when you hit it — no RTP protects you from chasing losses. Match volatility to your session: a 98% low-volatility pokie like Blood Suckers suits a long, steady sit; a high-volatility title suits a short, big-swing punt. And if a bonus is in play, confirm high-RTP pokies actually contribute to wagering, because some casinos exclude or reduce the weighting of the very games covered here. Combine a high RTP, low volatility and a fair bonus and you've done everything a player reasonably can — the house still keeps its edge, so treat every spin as entertainment.
