Gambling can be harmful

Pokies and online casinos are built to keep you playing. The odds always favour the house, and over time the maths means the average player loses. That's fine when gambling is a bit of fun you've budgeted for — a set amount you're happy to lose, like the price of a movie ticket. Kept in that box, it's a form of entertainment like any other.

It becomes harmful when it stops being a choice: when you chase losses, spend money you needed for rent or food, hide it from the people close to you, or can't stop even though you want to. Gambling harm affects money, relationships, work, sleep and mental health — and it doesn't only touch the person gambling. Health New Zealand estimates that each person experiencing gambling harm affects several others around them, which is why whānau, partners and children are counted in the harm too. It can build slowly, so many people don't notice until it's serious. It is not a sign of weakness, and it is treatable. Reaching out early makes it far easier to turn around.

Gambling is not a way to make money. No system, "strategy" or hot streak changes the house edge. If you're playing to win back losses or to solve a money problem, that's a warning sign — stop and talk to someone.

Why pokies and online casinos are easy to get hooked on

Pokies are designed around fast, repeated play and near-misses that feel like "almost winning". Online, there's no closing time, no travel, and money on screen doesn't feel like cash in your hand — so it's easy to play longer and deposit more than you planned. Bonuses, free spins and "one more spin" prompts all nudge you to keep going. Knowing how the games are built to hold your attention is part of staying in control; understanding return-to-player (RTP) and the house edge makes it clear that, over time, the machine is meant to win.

Warning signs to watch for

You might have a problem with gambling if you recognise several of these signs in yourself:

  • Spending more time or money gambling than you meant to, again and again.
  • Chasing losses — betting more to try to win back what you've lost.
  • Gambling with money you needed for bills, rent, food or family.
  • Borrowing money, selling things, or using credit to keep gambling.
  • Lying to whānau, partners or friends about how much you gamble.
  • Feeling anxious, guilty, irritable or restless when you try to cut down or stop.
  • Gambling to escape stress, boredom, loneliness or low mood.
  • Neglecting work, study, relationships or your health because of gambling.
  • Thinking about gambling a lot of the time, even when you're not doing it.

Signs in someone you care about

Warning signs in someone else are often quieter, because people hide gambling harm. Watch for secrecy about money, unexplained debt or borrowing without a clear reason, missing money or valuables, mood swings tied to wins and losses, being defensive when the subject comes up, withdrawing from work, study or things they used to enjoy, and being hard to reach for long stretches. One sign on its own may mean nothing; a pattern is worth a gentle conversation.

Tools that keep you in control

Every reputable online casino has built-in tools to help you set boundaries before you play, when your judgement is clearest. Set them up first, not after a bad night. Look for them in your account settings under "responsible gambling", "player protection" or "limits". Physical pubs, clubs and casinos in New Zealand have their own host-responsibility measures too — trained staff, visible helpline signage, no ATMs on the gaming floor, clocks and play-duration reminders.

Bank blocks and blocking software

Some of the most effective controls sit outside the casino, because they cover every gambling site at once:

  • Bank gambling blocks. Many New Zealand banks now let you switch on a block that declines gambling transactions on your debit or credit card. It often has a delay before it can be turned off again, which makes it a genuine circuit-breaker.
  • Blocking software. Tools such as Gamban and the free BetBlocker block thousands of gambling sites and apps across your phone, tablet and computer.
  • Budgeting help. MoneyTalks (0800 345 123) gives free, confidential budgeting advice if gambling has caused debt — a practical first step alongside counselling.

Self-exclusion in New Zealand

Self-exclusion is a formal agreement to keep yourself out of a gambling venue or site for a set time. It's free, and how it works depends on where you gamble.

Online casinos

At an offshore online casino, self-exclusion is done through your account — you choose a period (often six months up to permanent) and the operator closes access and should stop all marketing. Because these sites are separate businesses, an exclusion at one doesn't cover the others, so pair it with a bank block and blocking software to cover the whole internet at once.

Casinos (multi-venue scheme)

New Zealand's licensed physical casinos operate a multi-venue self-exclusion scheme: a self-exclusion arranged at one licensed casino is enforceable across all of them, including SkyCity Auckland, Hamilton and Queenstown, Christchurch Casino and Grand Casino Dunedin. Typical periods are six months, one year or two years. Staff are trained to help you set it up discreetly.

Pokies in pubs and clubs (Class 4)

For pokie (Class 4) venues, self-exclusion is arranged venue by venue with the duty manager and generally applies to that venue only — it doesn't automatically extend to other pubs, clubs or the casinos. If you play at several venues, you can exclude from each, and the Gambling Helpline can help you organise it. Complaints about how a venue handles host responsibility can go to the Department of Internal Affairs (0800 257 887).

Belt and braces. The strongest approach combines all three: self-exclude from the sites and venues you use, switch on your bank's gambling block, and install blocking software. Together they close off the easy routes back in.

A quick self-assessment

Take a moment and answer honestly. Over the last 12 months, have you:

  • Bet more than you could really afford to lose?
  • Needed to gamble with larger amounts to get the same excitement?
  • Gone back another day to try to win back money you lost?
  • Borrowed money or sold anything to get money to gamble?
  • Felt that you might have a problem with gambling?
  • Had people criticise your betting, or felt guilty about how you gamble?
  • Felt gambling has caused you health, money or stress problems?

If you answered "yes" to even one of these, it's worth a free, no-pressure chat with the Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655. Answering "yes" to several suggests gambling may already be causing harm — please reach out today. (These questions are based on the widely used Problem Gambling Severity Index; they're a prompt to talk to someone, not a diagnosis.)

Supporting a friend or whānau member

Gambling harm affects the people around the gambler too, and the same free services support family and whānau — you don't have to wait for the person gambling to reach out first. If you're worried about someone, you can help without waiting for them to hit rock bottom:

  • Pick a calm moment and talk privately, without blame. Say what you've noticed and that you care.
  • Listen more than you lecture. Shame and arguments usually push people away.
  • Don't lend money or cover debts — it tends to prolong the harm rather than fix it.
  • Protect your own finances and your family's — separate accounts and cards if you need to.
  • Point them to help, or offer to sit with them while they call the Gambling Helpline (0800 654 655).
  • Look after yourself. Call the Helpline or PGF for your own support — both help affected family and whānau directly.
Support for whānau. Mapu Maia (Pasifika), Asian Family Services and PGF all offer support built around the needs of the whole family, not just the person gambling. Reaching out for yourself is a valid reason to call.

18+ only — and keeping minors safe

Online casino gambling in New Zealand is for adults aged 18 and over (physical casinos are 20+). Reputable sites verify your age and identity, and it's illegal for anyone under 18 to gamble. Keep your login details private, and never let a minor use your account or watch you play as entertainment. If you're a parent, gambling-blocking software and device parental controls can keep gambling sites off shared devices.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, or you're having thoughts of self-harm, call 111 now, or free-call/text 1737 to talk to a trained counsellor any time.
