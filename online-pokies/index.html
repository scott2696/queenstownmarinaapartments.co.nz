What online pokies actually are

"Pokies" is the New Zealand and Australian word for slot machines. Online pokies are the same idea on a screen: spinning reels, symbols, paylines, and bonus features. You pick a stake, spin, and matching symbols across active paylines pay out. Every result is produced by a certified random number generator (RNG), so each spin is independent — the last spin has no effect on the next, and there's no "due" payout.

Modern pokies add layers on top: free-spin rounds, multipliers, cascading reels, "hold and win" mechanics and buy-a-bonus features. They're the most popular real-money game at almost every casino, which is why we treat the pokies library as a core ranking factor on our online casinos page.

RTP — the one number that tells you a pokie's value

RTP (return to player) is the percentage a pokie pays back over the very long run — millions of spins. A 96% RTP pokie returns $96 for every $100 wagered on average across its lifetime. The rest is the house edge. It's a long-run average, not a session promise: a single session can win big or lose fast regardless of RTP.

Jackpot & progressive pokies

A progressive jackpot pokie pools a slice of every bet, from every player on the network, into one prize that keeps growing until someone wins it. That's how the figure reaches into the millions. The trade-off is that the base-game RTP is usually a little lower, because part of every spin feeds the jackpot rather than paying back into the game.

The most famous example is Mega Moolah from Games Global (formerly Microgaming) — the pokie behind several record multi-million-dollar wins and the reason the Casino Rewards $1 offers at Zodiac and Captain Cooks send you straight to it. Other networks run their own progressives across NetEnt and Pragmatic titles. If you're chasing a life-changing win, a progressive is the only pokie type that offers it.

Are online pokies legal in New Zealand?

Yes — with a nuance. Under the Gambling Act 2003 it has never been an offence for an individual Kiwi to play pokies at an offshore online casino. What the Act restricts is casinos operating from within New Zealand, which is why the sites you use are based abroad. That is changing: the new Online Casino Gambling Act opens a licensed local market overseen by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA), which will issue around 15 operator licences by auction, with applications due 1 December 2026. Until licensed sites launch, offshore play remains the legal, practical option — and recreational winnings are not taxed.

Online pokies differ from the pub and club pokies you'll find in a New Zealand venue: those are Class 4 gaming machines regulated under the Act, with lower stakes and proceeds directed to community grants. Online pokies offer a far larger library, higher RTP transparency and the progressive jackpots covered above.
