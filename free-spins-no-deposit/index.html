What a "free spins no deposit" bonus actually is

You register a new account, confirm your email or phone, and the casino credits a set number of pokie spins — commonly on one featured slot. You didn't deposit, so the spins cost you nothing. Anything you win becomes bonus funds, and to turn those into withdrawable cash you complete the wagering requirement.

That is the whole game. The headline ("50 free spins!") is easy. The value lives in three numbers the casino would rather you skip.

The three numbers that decide if an offer is any good

  • Wagering requirement — how many times you must bet the winnings before withdrawing. "40× bonus" on a $10 win means $400 of bets. Lower is better; 0× (no wagering) is best.
  • Maximum cash-out — the most you can withdraw from a no-deposit win, often $50–$100 regardless of how much you spin up.
  • Game weighting & max bet — pokies usually count 100%, table games little or nothing, and a bet-size cap (e.g. $5) applies while wagering. Break it and you void the bonus.
