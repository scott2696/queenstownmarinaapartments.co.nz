What changed in 2025 for NZ sports betting

For years, New Zealanders could bet with dozens of offshore sportsbooks alongside the domestic TAB. That ended in 2025. Championed by Racing Minister Winston Peters, a law updating New Zealand's racing and betting framework passed Parliament with cross-party support in June 2025 and took effect that July. It made it illegal for overseas-based betting operators to accept wagers from people in New Zealand, reinforcing the TAB's status as the country's only legal bookmaker. The stated goal was to protect the betting revenue that funds local racing and sport — an industry worth well over NZ$1 billion a year, most of it flowing through the TAB.

  • The TAB is the only legal bookmaker for Kiwis, under the Racing Industry Act framework.
  • Offshore sportsbooks are barred from serving the NZ market — the duty sits on operators to refuse bets from verified New Zealand customers.
  • Punters aren't criminalised — the law targets operators, not individuals placing bets.
  • No open affiliate market — a single government-linked bookmaker means no competitive welcome offers to compare, and no honest "top 10 betting sites NZ" list to hand you.

Because enforcement rests on operators rather than internet-service blocking, some offshore sites remained technically reachable for a time. That does not make them legal to serve New Zealanders, and reputable books have withdrawn from the market rather than risk penalties. Is Betcha owned by TAB?

Yes. Betcha is a TAB NZ brand, not an independent rival. TAB's betting operations run in a long-term partnership with Entain — the UK-listed group behind Ladbrokes and Coral — and Betcha sits within that TAB-controlled structure. If you're weighing "TAB vs Betcha", you're really choosing between two front-ends of the same operator, drawing on the same licence, the same pools and the same responsible-gambling framework. There is no separate, better-priced book hiding behind the Betcha name. Read our full TAB NZ review for how the two brands compare in practice.

Fixed-odds vs tote betting

Two systems sit side by side. Fixed-odds betting locks in your price at the moment you place the bet, exactly like a traditional bookmaker. Tote (parimutuel) betting pools all stakes together and pays out a dividend based on the final pool and how many backed the winner, so your return isn't confirmed until the pool closes. Racing punters use both; most sports bettors stick to fixed odds.

Live and in-play betting

The TAB offers in-play betting on selected sports, letting you bet after an event has started with odds that shift in real time. Live markets are narrower than the offshore books Kiwis used to access, and turnaround on bet acceptance can lag — a recurring theme in TAB reviews.

How to bet on sport legally in NZ now

The legal route is the TAB — online at tab.co.nz, in the TAB app, by phone, or at one of its retail outlets around the country. Setting up is straightforward for New Zealanders:

  • Open a NZD account with the TAB or Betcha and complete identity verification (KYC) using a NZ address and ID.
  • Deposit from a NZ bank card or account — accounts are in New Zealand dollars, so there's no currency conversion.
  • Place your bet — pick fixed-odds or tote, confirm your stake, and the potential return is shown before you commit.
  • Withdraw to your linked NZ bank account — funds typically clear within one to a few business days.

NZ betting apps

The TAB app for iOS and Android is the legal NZ sports betting app. It covers racing and fixed-odds sports, live betting, results and account management, with responsible-gambling controls built in — deposit limits, self-exclusion, timeouts and betting caps. Is it safe and trustworthy to bet with the TAB?
