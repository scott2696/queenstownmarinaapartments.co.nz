Our verdict (3.9/5): The TAB is trustworthy, NZ-based, and the only legal way to bet on sport here — and betting with it helps fund local racing and sport. But being a monopoly, it has less pressure to sharpen odds or run generous promotions than the offshore books it replaced. Solid and safe, not competitive. Public review scores run low, largely on odds value, market restrictions and app performance.

What the TAB is

TAB NZ is New Zealand's government-linked betting operator and the country's statutory totalisator monopoly, established under the Racing Act. It ran the world's first government-operated totalisator back in 1951 and has traded under several names over the decades — the New Zealand Racing Board, then RITA, and now TAB NZ. Since 2020, and effective from June 2023, its betting operations have been run in a long-term partnership with Entain, the UK-listed group behind Ladbrokes and Coral. After the 2025 offshore ban it holds a monopoly on bookmaking for the NZ market, and its Betcha brand sits inside the same structure.

The scale is substantial: hundreds of TAB retail outlets nationwide, well over 100,000 account holders, and fixed-odds markets across dozens of sporting codes worldwide on top of its racing pools. Profits are returned to New Zealand racing and sporting codes, which is the public-interest argument behind the monopoly — the racing industry alone is worth well over NZ$1 billion a year and supports thousands of jobs.

Strengths

  • The only legal, NZ-licensed bookmaker
  • NZD accounts, local bank withdrawals, no currency conversion
  • Deep racing coverage and tote pools
  • Profits support NZ sport and racing
  • Established responsible-gambling tools

Where the monopoly shows

  • Less competitive odds than the old offshore market
  • Fewer and smaller promotions
  • Reported account limits on winning customers
  • No rival to switch to for a better line
  • App and product iterate slowly

Why the TAB has a monopoly

The monopoly isn't new in principle — only the TAB has ever been allowed to run sports and race betting for New Zealanders — but the 2025 law hardened it. Championed by Racing Minister Winston Peters, the change made it illegal for offshore operators to accept bets from people in New Zealand, closing the gap that let Kiwis wager with Australian and international books. The rationale was protecting the betting revenue that funds NZ racing and sport, which had been leaking offshore as online betting grew. The Entain partnership underpins that funding, channelling significant guaranteed payments to the TAB and, through it, to racing and sporting codes over the life of the deal. We unpack the full legal picture on our NZ betting law guide.

What you can bet on

  • Racing — thoroughbred, harness and greyhound; the TAB's core, with tote pools and fixed-odds, from NZ and overseas meetings.
  • Rugby union & league — All Blacks, Super Rugby Pacific, NPC, NRL, State of Origin and internationals.
  • Football, cricket, basketball — Premier League, Champions League, A-League, Black Caps, the NBA and other major codes with fixed-odds markets.
  • Big events — Rugby World Cup, Cricket World Cup, the Olympics and similar draw expanded markets and futures pricing.

Bet types and odds

You can place singles, multis, each-way and futures, with both fixed-odds and tote options on racing. Odds are shown in decimal format by default, the NZ and Australian standard. The recurring criticism — echoed loudly in public reviews — is that the prices aren't sharp: with no competing book to keep it honest, the TAB's lines are often shorter than the offshore market once offered, and some customers report restricted multi-bet options and capped stakes.

Account, app and payouts

You bet online at tab.co.nz, in the TAB app for iOS and Android, by phone, or at a venue. Accounts are held in NZD and funded from a New Zealand bank card or account; withdrawals go back to your linked bank and typically clear within one to a few business days. Because it's a NZ operator, there's no currency conversion and KYC is straightforward, though a first withdrawal may need identity verification.

How the app performs

The app does the job — betting, live markets, results and account controls all in one place — but it's a frequent target of complaints. Reviewers describe it as slow, occasionally clunky to navigate, and slower to iterate than the polished offshore apps Kiwis used to have. It's functional rather than best-in-class, which is what you'd expect from a product with no direct competition.

Responsible-gambling tools

The TAB app includes deposit limits, betting caps, timeout/curfew options and self-exclusion, so you can set guardrails before you play. These are genuine strengths of a regulated domestic operator — see our responsible gambling page for how to use them.

What real customers say

Independent review scores for the TAB are low — its public Trustpilot rating sits well under two stars, with the overwhelming majority of reviews at one star. The themes are consistent: odds described as poor value versus the old offshore market, restricted or unavailable markets (especially racing multis and some player props), accounts limited or capped after winning runs, and an app criticised as slow. Positive reviews exist but are a minority, praising helpful staff and easy access to results. Read alongside our own testing, the picture is clear — the TAB is safe and legal, but service and value are where a monopoly shows its edges.

TAB vs Betcha — same operator

Betcha is not a competitor to the TAB; it's a TAB brand inside the same Entain-backed structure. Choosing between them is choosing a front-end, not a better price. "TAB vs Sportsbet" is a non-question for the same reason — Sportsbet is an Australian book that can no longer legally take NZ bets, so it isn't a lawful alternative for Kiwis.
