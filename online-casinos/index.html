Casino bonuses explained — and the wagering catch

A welcome bonus is bonus money, not cash. Before you can withdraw winnings from a bonus, you clear a wagering requirement — the number of times you must bet the bonus (and sometimes the deposit) first. "35× bonus" on a $100 bonus means $3,500 of bets before a cent comes out.

  • Wagering multiple — lower is better. Under 35× is friendly; 50×+ is heavy.
  • Max bet while wagering — often capped (e.g. $5). Break it and you can void the bonus.
  • Game weighting — pokies usually count 100%, table games little or nothing.
  • Cashout cap & expiry — some bonuses cap what you can withdraw and expire in days.
Our rule: we never rank a casino on bonus size. A clear, low-wagering offer you can realistically clear beats a giant headline every time. If you only want spins with no deposit, start at free spins no deposit.

Is online casino gambling legal in NZ?

Yes — with an important nuance. Under the Gambling Act 2003, it has never been an offence for an individual New Zealander to play at an offshore online casino. What the Act restricts is casinos operating from within New Zealand, which is why the sites Kiwis use are based abroad.

That is now changing. The new Online Casino Gambling Act opens a licensed local market, overseen by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA). The DIA will issue around 15 operator licences by auction, with applications due 1 December 2026 and licensed sites expected to go live from 2026–27. Until then, offshore play remains the legal and practical option for players.

What this means for you: nothing you do today is unlawful. The open questions are about the operators — whether licensed NZ casinos will still allow $1 deposits, no-deposit bonuses and fast crypto payouts once the regime lands. We track that closely so you know what changes and when.

Do you pay tax on casino winnings in New Zealand?

No. For a recreational player, gambling winnings in New Zealand are not taxed — there is no income tax and no GST on a normal casino win, and it makes no difference whether the site is local or offshore. The rare exception is a genuine professional gambler running what amounts to a gambling business, who may be taxed on the profits. One more wrinkle applies to crypto: the win itself is tax-free, but IRD treats the coins as property, so a price gain while you hold them can be taxable.
