Quick answer: A casino bonus is extra value — bonus funds, a deposit match or free spins — that you claim to boost your play. Whether it's worth it comes down to three things: the wagering requirement (lower is better; 0× is best), the game weighting (pokies usually count 100%, tables little) and the max bet allowed while you clear it. A no-wagering offer is nearly always worth taking; a huge match with 50× wagering often isn't.

How does an online casino bonus work?

An online casino bonus is an incentive an operator credits to attract new players or reward existing ones. Claim a welcome bonus and the casino adds bonus funds or free spins on top of your deposit; those winnings are ring-fenced as bonus money until you clear a wagering requirement, after which they convert to withdrawable NZD. The exception is a no-wagering offer, where winnings are cash from the moment they land. Every bonus is governed by its terms and conditions — the wagering multiplier, game contribution rates, max-bet limit, bonus expiry and the maximum withdrawal cap — and those terms, not the headline size, decide whether the offer is any good.

How to read wagering, game weighting and max bet

Three settings decide how hard a bonus is to turn into withdrawable cash. Read them together, not in isolation.

  • Wagering requirement — the multiplier. "35× bonus" on a $100 bonus is $3,500 of bets before you can withdraw. If it's "35× (deposit + bonus)" the number is even bigger. Lower is better; 0× means no playthrough at all. NZ welcome bonuses commonly sit around 35×–40×.
  • Game weighting / contribution rate — how much each game contributes. Pokies almost always count 100%, but blackjack and roulette often count 10% or 0%. Play a low-weighted game and the wagering barely moves.
  • Maximum bet — the biggest single bet allowed while clearing wagering, commonly around $5. Exceed it — even once — and most casinos void the bonus and its winnings.
  • Bonus expiry — the window to clear the wagering, often 7–30 days. Miss it and the bonus and any winnings from it are forfeited.
  • Maximum withdrawal cap — a ceiling on what you can cash out from bonus winnings, most common on no-deposit and free-spin offers.
Do the sum first: Multiply the bonus by the wagering to see the turnover, then check the max bet, contribution rates and expiry. A "smaller" bonus with 20× and no cap often beats a "bigger" one with 45× and a tight cash-out.

What are casino bonus codes?

A bonus code is a short string you enter at registration or in the cashier to unlock a specific offer. Some bonuses credit automatically; others — especially exclusive or higher-value deals — need the code. Copy it exactly, apply it before you deposit where the offer requires, and confirm the bonus shows in your account before you play. Skip a required code and the casino usually won't add the bonus after the fact.

How to choose the best casino bonus — and tips to maximise it

The biggest number is rarely the best deal. Pick the offer that fits your play, then work it efficiently.

  1. Match the bonus to your play style. Pokie players get the most from free spins and pokie-weighted matches; table players should hunt low-wagering or no-wagering funds.
  2. Read the terms before you claim. Turnover, contribution rates, max bet, expiry and cap — all of it, every time.
  3. Do the wagering maths. Bonus × multiplier = required turnover. If it's unrealistic in the expiry window, skip it.
  4. Stay under the max bet and play only 100%-weighted eligible games while clearing the requirement.
  5. Complete KYC early so your first withdrawal isn't delayed.

Are casino bonuses worth it?

Sometimes. A bonus is genuine value when the wagering is low, the max bet lets you play normally, and pokies (which you were going to play anyway) clear it. A no-wagering offer is the clearest win — the winnings are cash the moment they land. Where bonuses turn sour is the high-multiplier match with a low cash-out cap: you can turn over thousands and still only be allowed to withdraw a fraction. If you value simplicity over size, no-wagering and low-wagering offers win.

When a bonus helps

  • Low or no wagering
  • Pokie-friendly game weighting
  • A workable max bet
  • Clear, generous cash-out cap (or none)

When to skip it

  • Wagering of 40× or more
  • Tight maximum cash-out
  • Very low max bet or short expiry
  • Wagering on deposit + bonus, not bonus alone

Casinos and bonuses to avoid

Steer clear of offers that pair a big headline with 50×+ wagering and a low withdrawal cap, bonuses that count your deposit toward turnover as well as the bonus, and any casino that hides its licence or lacks working responsible-gambling tools. If the terms aren't easy to find, treat that as the answer.
