NZ Betting Law Explained · Offshore Ban & TAB Monopoly 2026

New Zealand's gambling rules changed twice in two years, and the two changes point in opposite directions — betting got locked down, casinos got opened up. Here's the whole picture in plain English, whether online betting is legal in NZ, and what's legal for you.

Quick answer: Two separate things are true in 2026. Sports betting was locked down — a 2025 law banned offshore bookmakers and reinforced the TAB as the only legal bookmaker. Online casinos went the other way — the Online Casino Gambling Act is creating a licensed market, with the DIA making up to ~15 licences available and applications due 1 December 2026. Throughout, playing at an offshore casino has stayed legal for the individual under the Gambling Act 2003, and so has recreational winnings being tax-free.

The foundation: Gambling Act 2003

The Gambling Act 2003 is the backbone of the whole system, administered by the Department of Internal Affairs (DIA). Under the Act, gambling in New Zealand is illegal unless it is specifically authorised — and remote interactive gambling (gambling at a distance through a communication device such as a computer or phone) is prohibited unless it falls under an approved exemption like the TAB or Lotto NZ. Its key points for players:

  • Playing offshore is legal for the individual. Participating in an overseas-based gambling website is not an offence for a New Zealander — it never has been.
  • NZ-based online gambling was prohibited — a New Zealand company couldn't run an online casino for locals, so the market operated entirely offshore.
  • Advertising offshore gambling to New Zealanders was restricted, which is why you rarely saw casino ads on local TV or radio.
  • Class 4 gambling (pub and club gaming machines) and casino gaming are separately licensed and tightly controlled; operating unlicensed gambling carries real penalties.

That framework held for two decades. Then two laws changed the edges — one tightening betting, one opening casinos.

Change 1 — the 2025 offshore betting ban (TAB monopoly)

The 2025 law targeted sports and race betting. Championed by Racing Minister Winston Peters and passed with cross-party support in June 2025, it took effect that July and made it illegal for offshore bookmakers to take bets from people in New Zealand — reinforcing the TAB's monopoly, with its Betcha brand inside the same structure. Under the Racing Industry Act framework, only TAB NZ may offer sports and race betting to New Zealand customers; any other operator doing so breaks the law. The rationale was protecting funding for NZ racing and sport, which the TAB supports to the tune of well over a billion dollars a year.

  • Offshore sportsbooks — Bet365, Betfair, Sportsbet, Stake and others — can no longer legally serve NZ.
  • The TAB is the only legal bookmaker — see our TAB review and sports betting overview.
  • Enforcement sits on operators, who must refuse bets from verified NZ customers; the law targets operators, not individual punters.
  • The TAB's betting runs in partnership with Entain, which underpins the funding returned to racing and sport.

Change 2 — the Online Casino Gambling Act (licensing from Dec 2026)

Running the other way, this law opens a licensed online-casino market. The DIA will make available up to around 15 operator licences (no single operator holding more than three), with an application process due to close on 1 December 2026 and licensed sites following into 2027. For the first time, casinos will be able to operate legally for New Zealanders under NZ oversight, subject to binding conditions on player protection, harm minimisation, age verification (18+) and advertising.

What it means for you: until licensed sites launch, Kiwis keep playing offshore casinos legally. As licensing rolls out, expect NZ-regulated options to appear alongside them — we track what changes for deposits, bonuses and payouts on our online casinos page, and cover the full regime in the NZ online casino law guide.

Betting vs casino — don't confuse the two

It's easy to blur these together, but they are separate laws heading in opposite directions. Betting was locked to a single operator; casinos are being opened to a licensed, competitive market.

What betting sites can you legally use in NZ?

For sports and race betting, the legal answer is short: the TAB and its Betcha brand. They are the only operators permitted to take bets from New Zealanders. Offshore books like Bet365, Betfair and Sportsbet are barred, so "can I bet on Sportsbet in NZ?" has a clear answer — not legally. For casino games, the offshore market remains legal to play while NZ-licensed operators come online. If bonuses and fast payouts are the draw, that value now sits on the casino side, covered on our best online casinos NZ, crypto casinos and fast payout casinos pages.

Why is gambling a concern in NZ?

Part of the reason for both law changes is harm. Online gambling is fast, always available, and problem-gambling rates among online gamblers run far higher than in the general population. Offshore participation grew steadily over the past decade, and a share of every dollar spent overseas left New Zealand without local protections or funding. The betting monopoly and the casino licensing regime are both, in part, attempts to bring that activity under NZ oversight — with enforced deposit limits, self-exclusion and age checks as licence conditions. Whatever you play, treat it as entertainment and use the tools on our responsible gambling page.

Tax: the good news

Recreational gambling winnings aren't taxed in New Zealand — no income tax and no GST on a normal casino or betting win. The main exceptions are professional gamblers, for whom gambling is a primary income, and cryptocurrency, where the coin itself is taxable property even if the gambling win isn't.
