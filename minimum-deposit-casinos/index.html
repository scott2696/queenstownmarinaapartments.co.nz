What a minimum deposit casino is

Every casino sets a floor on how little you can pay in — its minimum deposit. At most sites that's $10 or $20. At a low-deposit casino it drops to $5, $3 or even $1. Low deposits pair naturally with jackpot pokies. A single dollar at Zodiac buys a set number of "chances" on a Mega-jackpot slot — you're not likely to win it, but the entry cost is trivial and the ceiling is life-changing.

The headline offer matters less than its terms. Check the wagering multiplier, whether it applies to the deposit plus bonus or the bonus alone, the game weighting, and any cap on winnings — a low-deposit bonus with a heavy playthrough can be worth less than a smaller, cleaner one.

Low-deposit casinos to avoid: red flags

A low deposit lowers your risk on paper, but a bad site can still trap it. Walk away when you see these.

Watch for:
  • No licence — an unlicensed or anonymous operator with no verifiable regulator is never worth a deposit, however small.
  • Unclear wagering — vague or missing playthrough terms, or wagering that quietly applies to deposit-plus-bonus rather than the bonus alone.
  • High minimum withdrawal — a minimum cashout well above your deposit effectively traps a small balance you can never realistically release.
  • Huge wagering multipliers — playthrough far beyond the market norm, which makes a "generous" low-deposit bonus almost impossible to clear.
  • Winnings caps hidden in the fine print — a low cap on what a bonus can pay out can gut the offer entirely.
