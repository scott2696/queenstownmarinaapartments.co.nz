Our Review Methodology · How We Test NZ Casinos & Bonuses

How we score: we rate every casino across six factors — payout speed, NZD & Kiwi banking, game range, bonus honesty, licensing & safety, and support & mobile. Each is marked 0–5, then weighted into an overall score out of 5. Payout speed and bonus honesty carry the most weight because they're where Kiwi players get let down most. Every score sits on a real logged withdrawal, and we re-check at least quarterly.

Our testing process, step by step

A review isn't a desk job. Before we score anything, a reviewer works through the same repeatable process on every site, so two casinos are always judged the same way. Here's the full sequence:

  1. Licence and safety check. We confirm the operator holds a valid licence, uses HTTPS encryption and RNG-certified games, and offers working deposit limits and self-exclusion tools. A site that fails the basics here doesn't get reviewed favourably no matter how good the games are.
  2. Register a real account. We sign up as an ordinary New Zealand player and complete identity verification (KYC) where it's required, noting how long it takes and what documents are asked for.
  3. Deposit our own money. We fund the account in NZD or crypto using the methods a Kiwi would actually use — card, bank transfer, Online EFTPOS or a stablecoin — and record any fees, minimums or declines.
  4. Read the bonus terms in full. If there's a welcome offer, we read the T&Cs end to end for wagering, game weighting, max-bet-while-wagering rules, winnings caps and expiry, and translate them into plain English.
  5. Play across the library. We try pokies, table games and (where offered) live dealer across several providers to judge range, quality and how RTP is disclosed.
  6. Test support. We contact live chat and email with real questions about NZD banking and payouts, and rate the speed and quality of the answers.
  7. Request a withdrawal — and time it. We cash out and log the time from request to money landing in NZ business days, including any pending or reversal window. Where we can, we run more than one withdrawal.
  8. Score, weight and write up. Each factor is marked 0–5, weighted, and combined into the overall score, with the reasons written into the review so you can see what drove the number.

We weight the factors toward what actually affects a New Zealand player's bottom line — getting paid, and knowing what a bonus really costs. Weights add up to 100%.

How the 0–5 scale works

Each factor is marked out of 5 in whole and half points:

  • 5.0 — best in the NZ market; nothing meaningful to fault.
  • 4.0–4.5 — strong, with only minor niggles.
  • 3.0–3.5 — solid but with a real weakness a player should know about.
  • 2.0–2.5 — usable but flawed; proceed carefully.
  • 0–1.5 — a serious problem (e.g. very slow or blocked payouts, predatory terms). We'll usually recommend avoiding.

We multiply each factor score by its weight and add them up for the overall rating out of 5 that you see on a review.

Real-money testing

We don't rate a site we haven't used. For every review we register a real account, verify our identity where required, deposit our own funds — in NZD or crypto — and play. Then we request a withdrawal and time it. The payout-speed score comes from that real, logged cash-out, measured in New Zealand business days, not from the operator's "24-hour payouts" marketing. Where we can, we run more than one withdrawal so a single fast (or slow) result doesn't skew the score.

Real testing is the only way to catch the things marketing pages hide: a "fast" casino that holds your first withdrawal for 72 hours behind KYC, a bonus that quietly caps your winnings, a support team that goes quiet the moment you mention a payout, or a banking method that's listed but keeps declining NZD cards. Because our reviewer is an actual customer using their own money, those problems show up in the score rather than staying buried in the fine print.

What we won't invent: we never publish an exact bonus figure, wagering multiplier or payout time for a named brand unless we've verified it or it's a well-documented offer. Where a number could change, we point you to the current terms rather than guess.

Quarterly re-checks — and after any terms change

A review is only useful if it's current. We re-check every site we cover at least once a quarter, and immediately whenever the operator changes its terms — a new wagering requirement, a changed payout process, a dropped banking method. Withdrawals get re-timed, banking options re-confirmed, and the "last updated" date on the page reflects the most recent check. If something has got worse, the score drops; if it's improved, we say so.

How we handle complaints

Readers tell us when a site does them wrong, and we take it seriously. We log every complaint, look for patterns across multiple players, and try to reproduce the issue in our own testing — a stalled withdrawal, a support team that goes quiet, a bonus term that quietly voids winnings. Confirmed problems lower the relevant factor score and are written into the review so the next player is warned. Isolated or unverifiable reports don't move a score on their own, but they tell us where to look harder.

Editorial independence

Our ratings are set by the editorial team from testing — full stop. No operator can pay for a higher score, a better ranking, or the removal of a criticism. Where the site carries a commercial link, it never touches the words or the number. If a site pays slowly or buries an unfair term, we'll tell you regardless of any relationship. That independence is the whole point of the score.

Like most review sites, we may earn a commission when a reader signs up through some of our links — that's how a free-to-read site funds real-money testing. We keep that separate from the scoring in two ways: the reviewer who tests and rates a site isn't the person who manages any commercial relationship, and a factor score can only move on the back of logged evidence from testing, not on the strength of a deal. A commercial partner can be criticised, marked down, or left off a list entirely, and several are. More on who we are is on our about page, and you can meet the reviewers on the authors page.

What a score doesn't tell you. A high rating means a site tested well for us at a point in time — not that it's risk-free, or right for you. Your own limits matter more than any number: set a budget, use the deposit and time tools, and read our responsible gambling page before you play.
